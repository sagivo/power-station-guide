DJI built a creator's power station, not a generalist's: 1024Wh and 2200W of AC wrapped around the best USB-C implementation in the category (dual 140W) and SDC fast-charging for DJI drone batteries — all at a whisper-quiet 23–26 dB.

*Check current price: [DJI Power 1000](amazon:dji-power-1000).*

## Who the Power 1000 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you fly DJI drones, edit on a 16" MacBook Pro, or otherwise live on USB-C. The dual 140W ports (280W combined) charge laptops and camera batteries faster than any rival here.

Skip it as a general home-backup box: no built-in MPPT (solar needs a bolt-on module), no expansion, only 2 AC outlets, and a 5-year warranty that trails the class only in paperwork. The [Anker C1000](/power-stations/anker-solix-c1000/) and [DELTA 3 Plus](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-3-plus/) are more flexible all-rounders.

## USB-C done right

Most stations offer 100W USB-C as an afterthought. DJI's dual 140W PD 3.1 ports are a genuine differentiator for creators — a MacBook Pro, camera batteries and a drone charging simultaneously, no AC inverter losses. The SDC ports take it further: DJI drone batteries fast-charge directly, skipping the AC adapter entirely.

## Real-world runtime examples

Usable capacity is ~85% of 1024Wh, about 870Wh:

- **MacBook Pro 16" (~140W while editing):** ~6 hours of timeline work.
- **12V camping fridge (~45W):** ~19 hours — a day off-grid.
- **DJI drone batteries (~60Wh each):** roughly 10–12 full charges — a long shooting weekend.
- **Camera battery charging (~30W):** ~29 hours — weeks of battery top-ups.

## The caveats

The missing built-in MPPT is the real compromise: solar charging requires DJI's add-on module, which is inelegant and easy to forget on a trip. And the 4000-cycle rating's 70% threshold means it can't be directly compared to the 3000-cycle/80% figures elsewhere in this guide.

## How it compares

Versus the [Anker C1000](/power-stations/anker-solix-c1000/): DJI wins on USB-C and silence; Anker wins on price, AC outlets, recharge speed and expandability. Versus the [DELTA 3 Plus](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-3-plus/): EcoFlow wins on AC output and growth path; DJI wins for the drone bag. On price alone, [best budget power stations](/posts/best-budget-power-station/) lays out the value math.

## The creator's kit, priced out

The Power 1000's pitch is the shoot, not the spec sheet: dual 140W USB-C for a MacBook Pro and camera batteries, SDC fast-charging for DJI drone batteries, 23–26 dB so it doesn't ruin audio. A wedding videographer can run two camera battery chargers, a laptop and a monitor (~250W) for about 3.5 hours — a full ceremony and then some — in near silence.

## What you give up

Two AC outlets is the tightest count here — power strips become mandatory. No expansion means 1024Wh is forever. The add-on MPPT module is a $100+ accessory that lives in a drawer until the one trip you need it. And the 4000-cycle rating's 70% threshold makes it look better on paper than it is versus 3000-cycle/80% rivals. Buy it for the USB-C and the silence; buy anything else for general backup.

## The vanlife creator's desk

This is the sleeper use case: a van or SUV desk where the Power 1000 sits silent under the bench. Laptop on one 140W USB-C, camera batteries on the other, a 27" monitor on AC (~60W), LED panel on DC — roughly 300W total, or about 2.9 hours of full-tilt editing per charge. Recharge from the wall in 70 minutes at the coffee shop, or 800W of roof solar (with the MPPT module) for ~1.3 peak sun hours. No generator, no noise complaints, no fumes in the living space.

## Bottom line

The Power 1000 is a specialist: the best USB-C and the quietest operation in the guide, built for creators who charge cameras and drones for a living. For general backup duty, the Anker C1000 or DELTA 3 Plus give you more flexibility per dollar.
