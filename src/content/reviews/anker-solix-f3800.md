The Anker SOLIX F3800 is the watts-per-dollar flagship: 3840Wh of LFP storage behind a 6000W split-phase inverter — the only portable box here that speaks 240V natively. Dryers, well pumps, EV mobile connectors and RV 50-amp service all plug straight in.

*Check current price: [Anker SOLIX F3800](amazon:anker-solix-f3800).*

## Who the F3800 is for (and who should skip it)

Buy it if you have 240V loads, want to trickle-charge an EV during outages, or want the deepest expansion path in the category (26.9kWh per unit). At sale prices under ~$2,200 it's also simply the cheapest way to buy 6000W of inverter.

Skip it if you need portability — 132 lb is the heaviest single unit we track, wheels or not. Skip it at list price; the $3,999 MSRP is fiction and the street price is the real price. And skip it if your backup plan is a single-day outage with a fridge: this much inverter is wasted there — the [Anker SOLIX F3000](/power-stations/anker-solix-f3000/) or [EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-pro-3/) fits better.

## Output: in a class of one

6000W continuous with 9000W surge, delivered as 120V/240V split-phase. No other portable station here does this from one box — the [DELTA Pro 3](/power-stations/ecoflow-delta-pro-3/) tops out at 4000W/120V (240V needs two units). Six 20A 120V outlets plus NEMA 14-50 and L14-30 cover every plug type a North American home or RV uses.

## Real-world runtime examples

Usable capacity is ~85% of 3840Wh, about 3264Wh:

- **Full-size fridge (~150W avg):** ~21 hours.
- **Window AC (~1000W):** ~3.2 hours of cooling per charge.
- **Well pump (750W cycling):** ~4 hours of actual pump runtime.
- **Space heater (~1500W):** ~2.2 hours — high-draw heat eats any battery fast, which is why the 6000W inverter matters: it can at least run the heater, unlike smaller boxes that just trip.

## Battery and expansion

The 3840Wh LFP pack is rated for 3000+ cycles (10+ year lifespan per Anker) and carries a 5-year warranty. Expansion is the deepest here: up to six BP3800 batteries (26.9kWh), or 53.8kWh with a second F3800. That's week-long outage territory with solar help.

## Solar and recharge

2400W of dual-MPPT solar input refills 0–80% in about 1.5 hours of strong sun with a big array. AC wall charging is slower (1800W max) — this is a solar-first machine. In practice, four 400W panels (~1400W real-world) refill the base unit in about 2.5 peak sun hours. Storm Guard mode in the Anker app watches weather alerts and tops the battery up automatically.

## EV and RV use cases

The NEMA 14-50 outlet is the F3800's party trick: plug a mobile EV connector into it and add roughly 15–25 miles of range per hour — enough to turn a dead-EV panic into a manageable afternoon. For RVs, the L14-30 outlet feeds 50-amp shore-power service directly, and 6000W runs the air conditioner plus everything else at once. These aren't marketing extras; they're the reason to pick this box over 120V-only rivals.

## What to buy with it

Budget for panels before expansion batteries. Four 400W rigid panels (~$600 street) use barely half the 2400W solar input and refill the base unit in about 2.5 peak sun hours. The expansion batteries are where the real money goes — watch for bundle sales, which often discount packs 20–30% when bought with the main unit.
