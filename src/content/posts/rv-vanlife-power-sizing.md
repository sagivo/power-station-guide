"How many watt-hours do I need?" is the most-asked question in vanlife power, and the answer is refreshingly calculable: list your devices, multiply watts × hours, add 20% headroom. Here's the math for the three most common setups.

## The 80/20 of RV loads

| Device | Typical draw | Typical use | Wh/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12V compressor fridge | 45W | 24h (cycles ~50%) | ~540 |
| LED lights | 20W | 5h | 100 |
| Laptop | 65W | 4h | 260 |
| Phones + USB bits | 20W | 3h | 60 |
| Roof vent fan | 30W | 8h | 240 |
| Starlink dish | 75W | 6h | 450 |
| Microwave | 1000W | 15 min | 250 |
| Induction cooktop | 1500W | 30 min | 750 |
| Electric kettle | 1200W | 12 min | 240 |

The fridge is the baseline everything else stacks on. Starlink surprises people — 75W × 6 hours is 450Wh, nearly a fridge by itself.

## Three setups, three answers

**Weekend warrior** (fridge, lights, laptop, phones): ~1,000Wh/day. A 1kWh station like the [Anker SOLIX C1000](amazon:anker-solix-c1000) covers a full day; recharge while driving home.

**Full-time vanlife** (add Starlink, fan, some cooking): ~2,500–3,500Wh/day. You want 2kWh minimum — the [Anker C2000 Gen 2](amazon:anker-solix-c2000-gen2) or [EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max](amazon:ecoflow-delta-2-max) — plus 400–800W of solar to break even on sunny days.

**Off-grid with cooking** (induction + kettle + microwave): 4,000–5,000Wh/day. Now you're in 3kWh territory ([Anker F3000](amazon:anker-solix-f3000)) with serious solar, or an expandable flagship.

## The AC question

Rooftop air conditioning is the load that breaks portable math: ~1,500W running, 3,000W+ starting, for 8 hours overnight = 12kWh. No portable station does that alone. If AC is non-negotiable, you're looking at a soft-start capacitor + the biggest station you can afford for a few hours of pre-cooling — or a generator.
