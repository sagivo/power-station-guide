Gas generators owned home backup for a century because nothing else stored enough energy cheaply. That math is breaking: a modern LiFePO4 station stores 3–4kWh silently, recharges from the sun, and costs less over five years than feeding a generator — for the outage profiles most households actually experience.

This isn't a "generators are dead" piece. It's the honest breakdown of which machine wins which scenario, with the fuel math.

## The 5-year cost math

A 7500W dual-fuel portable generator costs ~$900 upfront. At half load it burns roughly 0.75 gallons/hour. At $3.50/gal that's **$2.63/hour in fuel alone** — before oil, filters, spark plugs and your time driving to the gas station mid-storm.

Run the numbers for 120 outage hours a year (a bad-storm year in much of the US):

| | Gas generator | 3.8kWh power station |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront | ~$900 | ~$1,800–$2,200 (sale) |
| 5-yr fuel | ~$1,575 | ~$60 (grid top-ups) |
| 5-yr maintenance | ~$300 | ~$0 |
| **5-yr total** | **~$2,775** | **~$1,900** |

Under ~40 outage hours/year, the generator's cheap upfront cost wins. Above ~80, the station pulls ahead — and the gap widens every year gas prices rise. Plug your own numbers into our [5-year cost calculator](/tools/generator-cost/).

## Noise and livability: no contest

A portable generator runs 70–80 dB — a loud vacuum cleaner, all night, with HOA and neighbor implications. The quietest inverter generators manage ~60 dB. The [Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus](/power-stations/jackery-explorer-2000-plus/) runs at ~30 dB; the [DJI Power 1000](/power-stations/dji-power-1000/) at 23–26 dB. During an overnight outage, this isn't a luxury spec — it's the difference between sleeping and not.

## What each one can't do

**Stations can't:** run central air conditioning for days, power a whole house at 20kW, or conjure energy from nothing — once the battery and sun are spent, you're done until recharge.

**Generators can't:** run indoors or in attached garages (carbon monoxide kills), run quietly enough for overnight use near bedrooms, or start themselves at 2 AM without you going outside in the storm.

## The verdict: horses for courses — or both

- **Apartments, short outages, electronics, overnight:** power station, no contest.
- **Rare outages (<40 hrs/yr), tight budget:** a small inverter generator is the rational buy.
- **Multi-day outages, well pumps, heavy loads:** the biggest station you can afford *plus* solar — or a standby generator if the budget stretches.
- **Serious preparedness:** both. The station handles 90% of outage hours silently; the generator recharges the station and covers the worst 10%.
